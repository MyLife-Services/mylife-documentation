## Market Review/Opportunity

What is the Market?

At a broad level, we see at least three types of “Remembrance” Markets that exist today:

1. Deathcare/Funeral Products and Services
   - These services are currently, distinctly different from our offerings, and could perhaps be complimentary (See Customers section). We recognize no specific current major competitors in this space, although we could see large funeral homes and hospice services utilizing our services and/or potentially attempting to copy them for profit (see Threats section).
   - Even though funeral services and products would be affected by our technologies, we see those effects as industry improvements. Deathcare will benefit from:
     - Greater clarity as to the actual wishes of the deceased
     - New tools for modernized funeral services
     - Larger audience for modernized funeral services
     - Direct downstream familial consequences from the mental health benefits to elder care and grief counseling
     - Now partners in caretaking the personality of the individual whose remains they’ve processed

2. Research Repositories
   - Examples: Ancestry.com, Gramps.com and Wikipedia/ WikiTree. This market could be considered a subset of what we plan to offer. Currently this market is geared towards historical research and seems to be more data driven (vs emotion). This market could also be seen as complimentary to our effort, with the current players potentially being partners. Again though, we also need to consider some of them as potential competitors that may want to expand their offerings into our space.
   - Direct Remembrance Products. This market is the closest to our offerings. Although not fully developed, we see a few early ventures into this:
     - Historicity of self: Vehicles that allow users (as well as others) to contribute to their own story, to save for posterity. Storyworth or LifeTime Memories as examples.
     - Online memorial tributes: Websites that allow stories, quotes and photos to be shared with a digital community, some that allow virtual memorial services for loved ones. Forevermissed.com, GatheringUs.com, etc.
     - Digital identity cleaning or removal services, public post-relations for the little guy
     - Enduring trusts for digital resources and assets (permanent.org)
   - One obvious missing market here is Social Media, which has a tangential relationship to our offering. The data that exists (and that is always being updated) is certainly relevant to MyLife and could potentially be linked to our services. This is an area that will require a “soft touch” though to address data privacy issues, and to avoid drawing the attention of the major social media companies into our space. (see Competitors)

### Why does the opportunity exist?

At a basic level people want to be remembered. There are many existing markets supporting the idea that people don’t want to die, and if they must, they don’t want to be forgotten.

The market seems fragmented, with no concentration on providing a comprehensive remembrance product that allows both users and their loved ones to document an individual's story, through all available media (data, text, audio, video, etc.). And certainly, no product does this for free.

## Customers and Segments

Three broad customer groups have been identified for MyLife: Members (Users), Intermediaries and Sponsors

- Members – End users of our product
- Intermediaries – Partners and Technical Implementers of the MyLife platform
- Sponsors – Benefactors who will financially support MyLife

Note this Marketing Plan focuses on Members and Intermediaries. A separate Marketing Plan for Sponsors will need to be created at a later date.

### Members

Our initial focus for membership will be on the elderly/geriatric population, as well as relatives/caregivers of this group. We will strive to develop a messaging, marketing vehicle, internet, social, and PR strategy that is optimized to target this segment.

### Intermediaries

This customer segment includes all potential partners and resellers of our offering. It is believed that traction with our primary member base will be very difficult without utilizing at least some of these intermediaries at startup:

- **Elder Care**: The power players in this industry space are focused on complementary, non-overlapping infrastructures like housing, pharmaceuticals, and assistive devices.
  - Housing care and adult day care facilities would be expected to be entreated early and respond favorably, as any systematic investment of solitary time and reflection would be an extremely welcome activity to both patients and staff as well.
  - MyLife views assistive devices as potential collaborators, at a later date
- **Research Repositories**: Genealogy and ancestry companies, particularly those that currently offer free services (Gramps.com, WikiTree) should also be prioritized early in the marketing plan to take advantage of cross-link synergies.
- **Death Services**: Funeral homes and related services could be valuable resellers, but will likely need to need to approached later in the marketing plan after some traction has been gained via other sources.
- **Grief Services**: Because this segment focuses on behavioral health crisis counseling, MyLife would be primarily therapeutically beneficial in giving an individual something concrete to focus positive energy on in times of emotional difficulty. We see no conflict, in fact, to the opposite: we see a high degree of synergy with this segment.
- **Volunteer services and supporting systems** (care givers, churches, archivists, genealogists, etc), would be promoted with the aim of establishing a recognizable fleet of documentarians, be they motivated by compassionate, artistic, or archival inclinations

One early initial marketing strategy could focus efforts on 3 customer segments

- Elderly/Geriatric potential members (and their relatives/care-givers), Elderly Housing and Care Facilities, and Free Research Repository companies.
